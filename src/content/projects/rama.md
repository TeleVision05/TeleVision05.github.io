## What it does

RAMA is a vertical slice of a larger game, set in ancient India and drawn from the Valmiki Ramayana. You explore a 768 by 768 metre valley with a village, a river that falls over a cliff, shrines, forest and ruins. The story runs across the Five Kandas, the books of the epic, alongside three village quests. There are 18 characters and 10 landmarks to discover.

It is a release candidate that runs on macOS in Godot 4.7.2. A build and a gameplay video are on the [v0.1.0-rc1 release](https://github.com/TeleVision05/RAMA/releases/tag/v0.1.0-rc1). The clip and screenshots on this page were rendered off screen from the current code, with no sound.

## How it works

- Textures, sound effects, ambience and music are synthesised in Python with NumPy.
- Terrain, water, trees, buildings, monuments, animals, demons and weapons are generated as meshes in GDScript.
- The human characters use CC0 base meshes from Quaternius and animation clips from Kay Lousberg, credited in the repo.
- The world is hand-composed rather than pure noise. The river drops about 45 metres across the map.
- Combat has a hold-to-draw bow, a three-hit sword combo, a dodge roll and a parry. Keys 1 to 5 pick the arrow: a free standard shaft or one of four celestial arrows, the Divya Astras, which cost Prana.
- Automated suites drive the game headlessly to check loading, terrain, combat, quests, saves and frame rate. In a rough off-screen run on a busy laptop, CPU frame times averaged 41 fps across the map. I still need a proper benchmark on a quiet machine.

## What's next

- The macOS build is ad-hoc signed and not notarized, so macOS asks you to right-click and choose Open. I have only run the Apple Silicon half of the universal build.
- Distant villagers and enemies still need cheaper rendering to cut draw calls.
- Every arrow is selectable from the start, since nothing gates them behind the quests.
